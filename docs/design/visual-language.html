Planning Poker — Visual Language

Three directions for a new visual language, each in the three states that matter in the application. All three stay dark, all three run on the system font stack, and all three use the same CSS custom property names as web/src/app.css — the difference between them is a difference in values, not in code.

Decided: direction B, "Clear room", set in Geist. The other two stay in this document, because the reasoning behind the choice would not be followable without the alternatives that were rejected. Two changes to B against the first draft are already worked in: cards have only slightly broken corners at 6px instead of the initial 12px, and the row of cards along the bottom edge is drawn as actual cards — in the first draft that was a fault of this document, where they had collapsed onto their bare letters.

On the typeface. The specimens below still render in the system font stack, which is what the application does today; the switch to Geist happens with the implementation. Geist was chosen over Inter and IBM Plex Sans: it is the most matter-of-fact of the three, the smallest file at 29 KB, and its tighter setting suits the narrow cards. One candidate, Instrument Sans, was ruled out on a hard fact rather than on taste — it does not contain the ½ glyph, and the Fibonacci deck has a card that needs it, which would leave exactly one card in the deck rendered in a substituted typeface. Geist is licensed under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, so it may be bundled provided its licence text is bundled with it.

This file is a design document and not part of the delivered application. It lives under docs/, is therefore neither bundled nor served, and no visitor sees it. Authoritative for how the running application looks are web/src/app.css and the components alone; where this document disagrees with them, the code is right.

    Values as CSS
    

    



    
  
    Direction ATable lamp
    
  
    
    Keeps today's arrangement — dark room, green table, light cards — and sharpens it. Three
    interventions carry the effect: the base black becomes colder and deeper, the dividing lines
    become quieter (today, at #33415a, they are strong enough to speak rather than
    merely separate), and the accent moves from blue to a warm brass yellow. The table is no longer
    filled flat but lit from above.
  
    
  
    
    The reason for the change of colour: #4c8dff is the blue every framework ships with
    — it is not wrong, it is simply nobody's decision. Warm yellow on near-black reads as a lit
    table, and it gives the cream cards something to stand against instead of a second cold colour.
  
    
  
    
    
    Cost
    No new files, no new typeface. Implementable as a value swap in app.css plus pulling out the radii and shadows that are hardcoded today.
    
    
    Risk
    Yellow as an accent on dark has strong contrast, but yellow text on light surfaces would not — the accent may be fill and border, never type on white.
    
    
    Loss
    None that I can see. This is the most conservative of the three.

      Direction B — chosenClear room
      
  
      
    Takes the game metaphor out. There is no felt any more: the table is a slightly raised surface
    in the surface colour, with a fine rim and a hint of light along its top edge. The cards are
    pure white with a clear drop shadow, the back is a calm surface with a mark instead of a stripe
    pattern, and the accent is an indigo. The result looks like a tool, not like a card table.
  
      
  
      
    
      Cost
      A value swap plus a small addition in Seat.svelte for the mark on the card back. Here it is suggested by a spot of light.
      
    
      Gain
      The highest contrast of the three and therefore the best legibility; also the direction that ages the least.
      
    
      Loss
      The table as a table. That hits the paper balls and flowers flying across it in particular — the playfulness of that feature no longer has a place where it happens.
      
  
      
  
        
  
        
  
        Values as CSS
        

        



        
  
        Direction CCard room
        
  
        
    Goes the opposite way and commits to the game: a warm, dark room as if panelled in wood, real
    green felt with a fine grain and a brass edge, cream cards with a classic red back. The accent
    is brass. Of the three directions this is the only one with a handwriting of its own, and the
    only one in which a thrown paper ball looks like it belongs.
  
        
  
        
    
        Cost
        A value swap. The grain of the felt is two very flat crossed stripe patterns as a gradient — no image, no file, no foreign server.
        
    
        Risk
        Warm darkness is more prone to insufficient contrast than cold darkness, and beyond a certain strength the grain tips from "textile" to "dirty". Both are a matter of discipline during implementation, not of the direction.
        
    
        Gain
        Furthest away from "looks like every other tool".

          ObservationTwo things no colour fixes
          
  
          
    The table is empty while the round runs. The same effect shows in all four
    sections above: while people vote, the large surface holds nothing but the Reveal
    button and the line "Waiting for votes…". The space is reserved because the tally has to go
    there after the reveal and the table must not jump while it does — that is right, and
    RoomView.svelte says so explicitly. It still is the most noticeable weakness of the
    view, and it is a question of arrangement, not of palette. Changing it would be a proposal of
    its own, because it touches the table-ui specification; here it is only named.
  
          
  
          
    A and C are neighbours, B is the outlier. Honestly, Table lamp and Card room
    differ less than two names suggest: both keep the green table and a yellow accent. Their
    difference lies in the temperature of the room around it, in the colour of the card backs and
    in the grain. Choosing between A and C is choosing between two degrees of the same thing.
    Choosing B is choosing something else.
  
          

          


          
  
          NextWhat follows from the choice
          
  
          
    The chosen direction is implemented through the openspec flow: a change proposal with its
    reasoning, a design document, a task list and recorded verification. Only what must hold
    permanently — rather than what merely looks like this today — goes into the specifications under
    openspec/specs/: minimum contrast, the rule that the whole visual design is served
    from the application's own container, and respect for prefers-reduced-motion where
    there is movement. Individual colour values and corner radii do not belong there; they belong in
    app.css and in this document.
  
          
  
          
    The behavioural specifications are the acceptance criteria for this work and are not up for
    negotiation: seats stay on an ellipse around the table, the switch to the list below happens at
    58rem and narrower, a name of the full permitted length is shown whole, the deck
    stays reachable along the bottom edge, and no average is computed.
